Renovating a kitchen in Toowoomba

Toowoomba has plenty of kitchens ready for an update: older timber homes in East Toowoomba and Rangeville, post-war kitchens in Harristown, Wilsonton and Centenary Heights, and later family kitchens in Kearneys Spring, Middle Ridge and Highfields. Older homes can hide asbestos-containing materials, which must be handled safely.

Because the city sits high on the range, heating, insulation and draught sealing often come into a renovation conversation as well as the layout. In Queensland, check that the contractor holds a QBCC licence that covers the work, and that plumbing and electrical work is done by licensed trades. Removing a structural wall usually needs building approval.

Do I need council approval for a new kitchen?

Usually not if the layout and structure stay the same, but removing walls or other structural work can need approval, and plumbing and electrical work must be done by licensed trades. See our renovation process guide.

What licence should the renovator have?

In Queensland, the QBCC says work over $3,300 needs a written contract and home warranty cover, with deposits capped at 10% (under $20,000) or 5% ($20,000 or more).
